Bedroom Pop Pantheon

A new definition of "bedroom pop"; a collection of my favorites.

After writing my review of Gracie Abrams' debut EP *minor*, I felt that I had more to say about what's commonly called bedroom pop. Bedroom Pop has been around for a while, although its huge boost in popularity is largely attributed to Clairo, and her track 'Pretty Girl', created in her bedroom, with its now legendary lo-fi sound. You could fill a library with what's been written about this space - endless line-drawing, throat clearing, categorizing and re-categorizing of the same tracks over and over. Thus, I'd like to offer a different lens. Perhaps it's not always what's produced in one's room, but for where it's meant to be listened to. There are common qualities to the genre - a softness to the instrumentation, confessional lyrics, and a bittersweet somber tone. When considering the impact of these songs, it feels truer to me that we should measure against the tears dampening pillows in dimly-lit bedrooms than where the microphone recording the track was really located. With that established, I'd like to offer my Bedroom Pop Pantheon. In this essay, I'll touch on a selection of tracks which, through the product of their quality and immense popularity have defined a shared solitude: alone in your room; sharing the emotional impact with millions of other listeners.  

[clairo - bags](https://open.spotify.com/track/6UFivO2zqqPFPoQYsEMuCc?si=922c1aa03f7a47d9)

I'd like to open talking about Clairo - but not with Pretty Girl. Yes - Pretty Girl is the go-to citation for bedroom pop's rise in the late 2010s, and I've got a lot of love for its gentle vulnerability and her trailing off "I'll lose myself in you...". Nonetheless, I can't help but feel a much stronger pull towards the equally popular Bags, from Immunity. Where Pretty Girl's rounded, softer edges makes the track feel gentle and warm, Bags is much crisper and emotionally impactful. Trading the bedroom for a studio, the colder synths, clear percussion, and wispy vocals all amplify the impact of the song. The lyrics bite, too, with the highlights of "Sugar coated, melting in your mouth" and "Can you see me using everything to hold back?". Each atop rolling drums and synth stabs make for a very gripping listening experience. When I consider the story being told - I feel that this is really her perfect bedroom track. Alone after someone's just left - after you sugar coated half of what you wanted to say, and couldn't even choke out the second half - I imagine Bags was the song choice for thousands of people in this scenario. The wistful, bitter start to a tenuous crush seeps out of this song with every listen, and it is deeply compelling. 

[lizzy mcalpine - ceilings](https://open.spotify.com/track/2L9N0zZnd37dwF0clgxMGI?si=063b26675b42429b)

ceilings, under the traditional definition, is obviously *not* a bedroom pop song. I think the rich percussion alone disqualifies it, unless Lizzy's got quite a large room. Nonetheless, for me, it fits in seamlessly. I've spent many hours listening to ceilings while staring at my own ceiling, hearing raindrops blur my window and scatter the dim light coming through my window. I find binding these gloomy days to the narrative of "raining harder // my shoes are now full of water" to be deeply moving. The mixing on her voice is particularly fit to attentive listening - the cavernous, nearly echoing close-mic'd delivery simultaneously filling the soundstage and remaining intimately close in the listener's ear. At the start, a wistful portrayal, "you kiss me in your car // and it feels like the start of a movie I've seen before". As the instrumental swells and builds, we get the reveal that "it's not real // and you don't exist // and I can't recall the last time I was kissed". The rolling percussion, pulling strings, fading vocals - all the support the emotional collapse for the listener. This arc, even a hundred listens later, is deeply cathartic. I find that the 'you don't exist' is perfect for bedroom listening - lying in bed, constructing grandiose fantasies of how things could have been - before opening your eyes and coming back to earth. I am certain the ceilings was the soundtrack to this process for millions of listeners.

[billie eilish - ocean eyes](https://open.spotify.com/track/7hDVYcQq6MxkdJGweuCtl9?si=01f853a5ffd54903)

ocean eyes is probably the canonically true "bedroom pop" song of the bunch - a repurposed demo, an unexpectedly colossal success. For me, though, it's most special because of the vocals. With such a simple instrumental, her vocals have to expand to fill the space. And they do so well - the layering, echoing whispers. The second vocal run under "No fair // No fair". The reversed wisps of the chorus atop the vocalizations at the end. In contrast, her vocals soar and glow above the instrumental, which is very simple. The steady bass stepping up and down, the clean and spacious percussion. I particularly appreciate how much melodic weight is carried by her vocals - where ceilings' instrumental might swell, here, it's Billie's voice. The instrumental is held - with minimal attack, and instead the throes of her voice - the vibrato in 'cry'; the arc in 'ocean' - progress the track. I particularly love the idea of fear and uncertainty conveyed here - "I'm scared // I've never fallen from quite this high" - of course talking about falling in love. This reflection on fear in tenuous love is perfect for a late-night listen curled up in ones own bed.

[phoebe bridgers - motion sickness](https://open.spotify.com/track/6LxcPUqx6noURdA5qc4BAT?si=9628e6a5adf242ee)

As with the rest, Motion Sickness doesn't really fit - its guitar-forward presentation and wide soundstage immediately disqualify it from being traditional "bedroom pop", it might not even be pop at all. Despite being the least obviously fiting, I think it's the most hollowing of the bunch. Right from the opening - "I hate you for what you did // And I miss you like a little kid", over the rippling guitar strums is painfully gripping. There are few songs that move me like Motion Sickness. Where other songs feel like they pull at the heartstrings, this one feels more like it rips my heart out. I particularly love the imagery: "while you're bleeding on your back in the glass // I'll be glad that I made it out // and sorry that it all went down like it did". At the end, she notes - "I want to know what would happen // If I surrender to the sound". I find that, listening to this song, that I can't help but surrender to the sound. Laying in my room, letting the guitar wash over me, and just sitting with the emotions that arise, I feel that surrender is the right word. Once you've screamed and cried until you just can't any more, surrender is what's left. Motion Sickness accompanies it perfectly. 

[gracie abrams - i miss you, i'm sorry](https://open.spotify.com/track/4nyF5lmSziBAt7ESAUjpbx?si=3b21de909d384e2e)

 On my discussion of this track in the minor review - I focused on the instrumental arc, praising the dynamic instrumental, earned grandiosity, and rich vocals. Though these qualities would easily disqualify it from being a normal "bedroom pop", I think they make it perfect for this shared-solitude phenomenon. I find it captures the range of nighttime introspection - from her small, confessional "But I miss you, I'm sorry", to the painful waves of "I don't wanna go, think I'll make it worse". The repeated, unyielding obsession - "You said forever, and I almost bought it"; "Everywhere I go leads me to you". Often, people have an optimistic view that breakups are supposed to cleanly sever a connection between people. Here, Gracie captures not only how false that is, but the associated guilt, with the titular - "I miss you, I'm sorry". This progression - though huge and dynamic - is fit best for when you feel very small, and alone.

Across all of these tracks, there is something special in their popularity. When I listen to this sort of music, I can't help but think that Phoebe Bridgers said it best - there's no place like my room. Often, music is a shared experience in the literal sense - a party, a club, even a car ride - where everyone's listening to the same thing. This is a different version where, although the music is engaged with alone, this experience is mirrored to millions of listeners. From this emerges a shared solitude: a personal, introspective, cathartic listening experience, mirrored with thousands if not millions of other listeners. To discuss the tracks here with friends, both of us passionate about the emotional impact and reflection of our own lives, bonding over songs that we both adore but have never listened to together - is really special, and it's why I hold this genre in such high regards.

This is just a small selection of the tracks in this space that I love. Attached is a playlist of many more. I hope that you can find some of the same catharsis as I - and many others - have found in these songs.
