U
underscores

underscores is an artist I've been following for a long time. Since finding [moniker](https://open.spotify.com/track/45045fi8RzRxvU9bImb6xp?si=8e8e681b62994ed7) in 2020, I've followed along for the hyperpop highs of fishmonger, the worldbuilding indie rock of Wallsocket, and now, her foray into clean electropop - U. After the success of Wallsocket, I expected a further venturing into rock - perhaps an underscores Census Designated moment. Instead - we got a consistent and remarkably effective pop record that's exciting from end-to-end. More than just being enjoyable to listen to - this album is infused with life; it feels very vivid. U transforms from being everything I could ask for in a pop record: danceable, passionate, energetic, romantic - to a viscerally moving listening experience. 

April has a certain swagger that makes for tracks that just captivate you. When I saw her open for Danny Brown before this album's release, she ended her set holding a light bar - illuminating her silhouette as smoke filled the stage. Sure - it's aura farming - but she's also just effortlessly cool. I get this sense from Do It !SM, with its paired bass hits, dense percussion, and bouncy tempo. I particularly appreciate the lyrics in the second verse - the "Don't you get it?" with the second "(Don't you get it?)", as if said with a raised eyebrow and shake of the head. She continues - "I'm tryna run a business here, come on babe // Mm, don't take it personal ('cause listen)" before it drops back into the chorus. The flirty detachment, the subtle flexing, and the air of just being plain cool all enhance an already enjoyable track. This song feels like a foil to the later Innuendo (I Get U) !SM - with its similar pounding bass and intimate vocals - but the opposite message. There, we can see through the facade a bit - "Sometimes I feel like I'm a knockout // But I'm way too easy of a target now". Instead of being the one who gets whatever she wants, there's a tenuous desire with "Part of me is hoping I get // You". We get the full range here - from coy and aloof to tense and vulnerable - and both draw me further into the album.

Other times, this album is just plain fun. When I listen to the first single to release, Music !SM, I just want to dance. That stuttering bassline, straightforward chorus, and glowing melodic break under the titular "Music!", all work well together. It's safe to say that I too "feel the BPM // I feel the music". The second half of the track is even better - the frenetic build under the repeat of "pop, rock, electronic, rap, rock and roll", before the huge drop. Over the soaring melody, she sings "It's everything, it's everything to me", and I just want to scream along. I'm not sure that she and I have the same relationship to music - obsession with producing it as opposed to consuming it - but I couldn't agree more that music is everything to me. It feels that, in the middle of a densely arranged, easy listening electropop track, there is a transparent window from audience to artist. The repeated, almost pleading urging that "it's everything" is really gripping. At times, it moves me nearly to tears - seeing her pure passion for music borne out in such an exceptional album. Music immediately follows into Hollywood Forever !SM, another absolute heater. I adore the punctuated repeats of "I'm so- I'm so" and "all so- all so" of the chorus - which aids the transition from straightforward pop song to electropop breakdown. It starts with the build of "Don't you wanna come be famous with me?" - amplified by punchy electronic synths under the refrain - "aren't we all so Hollywood?". It peaks at the end of the track - the ripping, distorting bass like a chainsaw, leading into the bubbly echoing of e-ever - super fun. Finally, the track's denouement - the breathy, fading wisps of "You don't believe, believe in me". A roller coaster of a track - but undeniably a banger.

As the album progresses, this radiant energy is replaced with a more tense intimacy. On Bodyfeeling !SM, it's easy to follow the story being told - "we're in lockstep // It's cosmic". Although I do like the sentiment, I find the first half of the track to be a bit unengaging. Though I like the instrumental tone, and nice rhythm, it feels as if it wanders a bit. Moreover - I find "bodyfeeling" to be clunky phrasing, so it's unfortunate that it's the anchor. That said - when the track hits its apex - it hits hard. The repeated, passionate delivery of "What makes you think // I can't still love you?" over four-on-the-floor kicks, rich guitar, and crashing cymbals is immensely effective. If the first half is "a body feeling I keep dancing around" then the second half is refusing to avoid it any longer, and finally making it real. For me, this instrumental swell redeems the track. Continuing the love story into my favorite track on the record, The Peace !SM, we get a glimpse of a budding romance over chopped vocals and impossibly rich bass. At first - there's just the vocal melody - "smoke number one, I was in Brooklyn // To you I'm just a stranger". It builds - as the bass starts to come in - "smoke number two was at Coachella // Selfishly, I wanted you there, even if I made it worse". Finally, the bass gets even stronger, pushing the instrumental to its climax under "smoke number three I was in Europe // Left you the pack and found the perfect way to word it". A shared escape, a kiss-by-proxy, a growing love told through vignettes - I can't get enough of this track. Especially live - the pulsing bass became physically visceral and immensely emotionally gripping. On both these tracks - the heated tension of the other tracks burns to a smoldering yearning, which I really love.

Eventually, this blossoming love becomes a missed timing, a post-breakup recovery - where she is at her most vulnerable. Where I found Bodyfeeling to be a bit clunky, I actually like Lovefield !SM, even as a coined term. The idea of reaching out, hands outstretched, trying to pull another into your lovefield, but just being unable to - is quite poignant. She dotes: "Wish I could get it but I can't, I can't - you're just outside the Lovefield". Later, a brutal introspection - "Is the lovefield really getting smaller? I don't want to be untouchable anymore." I am always a fan of songs like this: hollowing, downtrodden lyrics; bouncy instrumental flourishes. Even the spelling hook - a trope often found in the most accessible, upbeat pop - accompanies the resignation that "you're just outside the lovefield". These elements contribute to the inherent contrast of the album - the back-and-forth, the outward confidence and inward vulnerability, the "will they won't they". This comes to a head on the closer, Wish U Well !SM - where instead of pulling someone into the lovefield, she reflects on someone who left it long ago. I'm always compelled by the idea of grappling with an ex-lover's current partner, and April's version of this demonstrates why. At the end of the first verse, she describes how "She looks just like me // In a way, it's beautiful // I'm wherever you go, but I" - into the chorus of "I'll wish you well forever". As she continues, the facade slips - "Can't let you go // My picture-perfect one and only // I can't go back to you, and I know it" - over slamming bass and rapid percussion - emphasizing the emotional impact. Finally, the whole instrumental is replaced with a single guitar, as she confesses: "If I'm being honest with myself, I don't want closure // I want to feel the gravity of losing you." I'm so moved by the choice of "gravity" - an unyielding force, pulling you down to the ground, or back to earth when your head's in the clouds. A beautiful, albeit hopeless, note to end on. 

With such a powerful ending, I'm left realizing that U isn't what I expected, not even close. That said - I am not remotely disappointed. Where I might have expected eccentric hyperpop, dense rock music, or something unlike anything I'd heard before, instead I got U - a nearly frictionless pop album. As a creative foray into a brighter, more accessible sound, U is undeniably successful. More than that, though, it's a success because of how much of her life is packed into this record. What at first listen feels like fun, easy listening becomes almost confessional by the end, with vignettes of her could-have-been romance left in pieces. What makes this album special are the transformations: from love to loss; from disconnected to vulnerable; and from radio-ready single to confession directly to the listener.
