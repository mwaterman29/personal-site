minor
Gracie Abrams

With my receipt of the five-year anniversary vinyl, I thought it an apt time to return to Gracie Abrams' first EP - *minor*. In my review of [*The Secret of Us*](https://www.mwaterman.dev/music/Gracie%20Abrams%20-%20Good%20Riddance.md), I wrote about the pain and disappointment of an artist gone mainstream. In my review of [*Good Riddance*](https://www.mwaterman.dev/music/Gracie%20Abrams%20-%20Good%20Riddance.md), I wrote about my deep appreciation for her vulnerability, and how it has enriched my love of the genres she occupies. Despite it being her debut release, *minor* sits somewhere in the middle, having both exceptional sonic quality and disappointingly generic choices. I'll spend most of this review praising the EP, and end on why that praise leaves me unsteady.

The obvious standout quality throughout is her voice. Comparing it to her later work, there's a youthfulness to her voice, fitting given the theme of the album. A bit higher register, a bit weaker delivery, but the same breathy quality for which she's now known. On Long Sleeves !SM, I enjoy the layering on "You didn't think... you didn't think", or the slight shakiness at the end of "Could you please pretend that it was only wind?" Even on the impassioned, echoing "Got a lot to work through", it smolders to a whisper. Similarly excellent is Under / Over !SM, where her voice sounds a bit compressed, warmer, even fuzzier. It's perfect for dwelling on memories, with her account of "swimming in the memories, and floating in the deep end". The progression from "I'm underwater, but I'm over you" to "I'm underwater, am I over you?" is short and gentle, just a shred of doubt to let the verse fall away. It ends on fragments of her voice, stuttering and echoing. It is truly a shame she never brought this effect back - I adore chopped vocals, and who better than my favorite vocalist? It's particularly fitting for being underwater - like light rays through the water, or a distorted rippling reflection, there are just shards of her voice swirling around the listener. 

Her subdued, whispery voice builds on the themes, too - as often Gracie's music feels small, pathetic, even confessional. On the title track, minor !SM, the lyrics capture this juvenile frustration with life - "I would drive all night to get to you // but my curfew is early and mom's up at home". Strict parents, a local drive, a small life lived in a bubble - all quintessential to growing up. I love the fully whispered "but you gotta understand I can't" - grappling with agency over a subdued instrumental and sparse percussion. I feel like my whole life as a kid was wanting more space, more agency, wanting to be older and more respected - so I appreciate sharing this struggle with her. Although I find the instrumental to be boring, Friend !SM is another look back on being younger: "Pictures of the old us got me feeling older". The post-breakup imagery is weaker than her later work - "funny how you feel like we would ever talk again" doesn't come close to Full Machine's "I'm a forest fire, you're the kerosene" - but as an answer to "can we still be friends?", the lyrics fit a first breakup. Beyond the themes, there are small details that have me coming back to the track, even despite the relatively generic guitar work. The vibrato in "I'll only see all the things that I'm missing", the delivery of "Killing me a little bit" - especially the echoed copies later in the track, these finer points keep me returning. Throughout the EP, her voice complements the storytelling and instrumental elements, and effectively grapple with what it's like to be young.

Where all these elements come to a head is on the standout track, I miss you, I'm sorry !SM. Although I think the true 'generational' bedroom pop song may belong to Clairo's Pretty Girl, Phoebe's Motion Sickness, or Lizzy's ceilings, this is certainly a contender. As a burning, wistful breakup song, I am amazed by this level of quality achieved so early in her career. Her delivery is precise, rich, and passionate where it needs to be, going from the somber "is that still true?" to the searing "every corner of this house is haunted". As it continues to build, the instrumental scaffolds her voice - heavier kicks under stacking vocal layers. Finally, it all falls away as she confesses "But I miss you, I'm sorry", before the colossal second half. The chorus echoes against "I don't wanna go, think I'll make it worse". Despite being much larger, more grandiose than the other tracks here, it does not feel stilted, even for a second. I often find that mismatched gravitas or unearned impact can absolutely ruin a song, but that's not the case here. It starts slow, hits a strong climax, and ends gently, with the final, falling "everywhere I go leads me back to you" as a seamless denouement. As a viscerally gripping portrayal of a relationship - and the guilt of still being attached after it's over - it's easily the strongest track on the EP, and deserves the immense success it has achieved. 

With all that in mind, I still feel as if I have picked an imperfect artist to fall in love with. She truly is my favorite vocalist - an effortlessly textured voice; an intoxicating whisper. It takes to layering unbelievably well - whether it's close-mic'd or filling the soundstage. And although I meant every word of praise I gave this album above, at times, it feels hollow. Consider the wispy chopped ending of Under / Over that I otherwise love - I said it's a shame that she didn't bring it back. I think she didn't bring it back because it's not the track that garnered hundreds of millions of plays. Looking at it through this lens, it seems like she might just be aping Cavetown edits. The variety of sounds and rough edges displayed here feels less like a young artist refining her sound, and more like someone seeing what's going to stick. 

Even her voice, which I adore, carries an undertone of imitating Lorde. When I went back to Lorde's feature on [Magnets](https://open.spotify.com/track/7nRmfGNhHKEEu5o8yFXLXt?si=f2cdf3745d234047) - I legitimately forgot it *wasn't* a Gracie track. This isn't the usual "ongoing cultural conversation", it's imitation for the purpose of finding an audience. I'll never stop loving the sound of her voice, but I do have to grapple with why she sounds like this. For me, that is the difference - an artist refining one's own artistic vision, versus an artist seeing what will get the most people to click play. Yes - *minor* is a compelling exploration of a young Gracie's life - struggling relationships, chained to her hometown. The other side of this EP, though, is the foundation for a commercial engine - one that's likely to produce immense streaming numbers, but unlikely to produce another Long Sleeves, Under / Over, or I miss you, I'm sorry. I can only hope that the upcoming release of *Daughter From Hell* is a return to form for her, rather than seeking another big hit, although I fear the commercial engine is already taking over. Seeing that That's So True - unequivocally her worst song - is now at over a billion plays, I think she might have gotten what she wanted. I'm not sure that it's what I wanted, though.
