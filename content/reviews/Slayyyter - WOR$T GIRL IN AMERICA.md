WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA
Slayyyter

Before this album came out, my latest touchpoint for Slayyyter was [Out Of Time](https://open.spotify.com/track/15vhXTUpPHEonzASrSwRjp?si=8f6904f87af941a9), a soaring, frictionless electropop song. I had assumed she'd push further in this direction - another round of neon magenta synthwave to accompany late-night cruising. I could not have been more wrong. Instead, out of nowhere, we got one of the most enjoyable electroclash albums ever. Here, she picks up the mantle of clashy percussion, EDM influence, slamming bass, and chopped lead vocals. I'm delighted to see another addition to the growing neoelectroclash canon. Bringing exceptional songcraft and pop chops into a new direction, Slayyyter delivers an incredible series of tracks that, beyond being infused with her energy and personality, hit such high highs that they're impossible to get out of your head. 

It's probably easiest to talk about this album through its bookends, starting with the opener, DANCE... !SM. On my year-end list for [last year](https://www.mwaterman.dev/music/essays/Top%2010%20Songs%20of%202025.md), I opened with kmoe's [Carpet](https://open.spotify.com/track/5DTqcZlVd7HmXxRAIcWKZo?si=KEgQOB57SlSRUHjldkD93g), analyzing how the combination of a spacious, buzzy synth with restrained vocals creates a suffocating feeling. DANCE sounds similar at first - straightforward percussion, sawtooth synths, and her crisp vocals cutting through the noise. However, where Carpet suffocates, DANCE radiates aura - there is a certain swagger to the track that's just intoxicating. This detached disposition draws you in as the Pretty Woman effect leaves you wanting more: "I kinda hate you but // It doesn't matter, let me dance // You seem so miserable // It doesn't matter, let me dance". This swagger, carried throughout, starts to crack by the closer, BRITTANY MURPHY. !SM On the track, she narrates her own funeral, her voice pushed to wisps on "Do you notice... all I've done?" On the final verse she is still just too cool, even posthumously. "Make sure they got red roses, I'm old fashioned // Make sure you book a good band to get it crackin' // Too busy to hang next week, so quit asking". This song's high rating was cemented for me the first time I heard it - her slight chuckle under "get it crackin", despite being a minute detail, infuses the song with so much personality. Where so many artists refuse to break the facade, Slayyyter instead asks: "Is my face too disgusting for open casket?" I simply adore the juxtaposition of playful, self-aggrandizing, "nah we can't hang" with the narration of her own suicide and questioning of how she'll be remembered. It takes a real confidence to pull apart one's own legacy like this, and this vulnerability makes the whole album drastically more engaging - especially contrasted against the ostensibly just-too-cool persona she portrays. 

The instrumentals, beyond working to scaffold her voice, also energize the listener. Nearly every track makes me want to get moving. For example, on OLD TECHNOLOGY !SM, the most straightforward banger on the tracklist, the energy comes from the contrast between the melody and her vocals. The beat is enjoyably direct - pounding hits and clean rhythm. Cutting through this, her voice is like sour candy, with the repeated, high-register, drawn-out "old technology!". I think the chorus of this song could get almost anyone to dance. Even more dynamic is CRANK !SM, with its pulsing bass and emphatic delivery. Although this song is popular for its raunchy lyrics - and their comical reference to "I get so gay off that tequila!" - the production is what deserves praise. She commands: "CRANK IT!", followed by this haunting, overbearing, driven synth. It is like a mix between a revving engine and the sound of being hunted in a horror movie, which is viscerally gripping for the listener. As the song comes to a climax, her voice echoes against the panning screams and chorus of "Crank, crank it!" over slamming bass hits. There is no melody, just the bass, her screams, and that nightmarish synth that makes the song unforgettable. 

Energetic instrumentals, rich vocals, and perfect composition make for a great album, and if it were just that, I'd still applaud it. Instead - she chooses to go even further. The same effect that makes CRANK so good - pushing a track to its perfect form by the end - is what really defines the highlights of this album. The gritty lead single, BEAT UP CHANEL$ !SM opens with blown-out vocals, muddied bass, and barely any melody at all. Then, under the chorus of "I want beat-up Chanels, I want new personnel", a buzzing synth starts to construct a melody. This builds and builds, until the huge flourish at the end - where the synth warps to become this incredible, searing top melody. The transformation from a sleazy club banger into this euphoric crescendo elevates the track immensely. The true zenith of this album, though, is YES GODDD !SM. Immediately, the guitar feedback, echoing vocals, and howled "YES GOD!" over that ripping synth all combine to reach the highest point yet. Again, she doesn't stop there, as the chorus swirls into the breakdown - it feels like a perfected version of stilted brostep drops from a decade ago. Imagine taking the drop from [Dance to It](https://open.spotify.com/track/6lykIUj47iMEB7HJlPIpYb?si=33fdaaec2c414e99), blowing it out, chopping it up, and weaving it into this screeching electroclash track. It feels like a darker, more energetic reprise of the music I grew up on, and I adore it for that. On both of these songs, she takes what is already an incredible track, and pushes it to such an apex that it becomes impossible to put down.

Beyond the individual tracks, it's refreshing to see an artist pursue innovation and new sounds without feeling like genre tourism. In my previous review of Gracie Abrams' [latest album](https://www.mwaterman.dev/music/Gracie Abrams - Daughter from Hell.md), I excoriated her for relentlessly pursuing commercial interest, even at the cost of her own sound. This album is completely the opposite - staying true to her vision as an artist, and honoring her influences. Slayyyter absolutely pulls from other places - the fringes of neoelectroclash, 2012-era EDM, indie synthpop, and so on - but she pushes these sounds forward, and makes them her own. Consider OLD FLING$ !SM - with its airy vocals over lush pads that could fit in among the indie pop highs of M83 or MGMT. Instead of traipsing through the tropes of what's popular at the moment, she takes a sound that's fifteen years old, and refines it. Replacing the blown-out "woo-hoos" of Midnight City with swirling fragments of her vocals creates an enjoyable song that by no means sounds a decade out of date. I am deeply impressed with Slayyyter's songcraft here, and I applaud her for being productive, not reactive, especially given how easy it would have been to cave to what's popular. 

Having thoroughly praised this album for its songcraft, I'd like to end on a more personal note. This spring, I was visiting a friend in Chicago for a long weekend as he was finishing up university. He dragged us to the clubs, and I thought to myself: we'll go out, we'll dance, maybe we'll even hear the new Slayyyter album. As the night progressed, and the DJ started to play some good songs, my friend grabbed my phone and queued OLD TECHNOLOGY on Spotify, hoping it'd play. Of course, queueing the song doesn't actually do anything - it just goes to my earbuds... or so we thought. A few songs later, it actually did play. As soon as we heard the first bouncing synth, we went absolutely ballistic. I will never forget that.
